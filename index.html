Overview

In 2024 the field asked whether we could build robot foundation models. RT-2, Octo, and Open X-Embodiment proved the concept. In 2025 the question shifted to real-world viability: π0 folded laundry, GR00T N1 controlled humanoids, and OpenVLA put a 7B generalist policy in reach of any lab. The harder question now is the same for all of them. Can they run fast enough, cheaply enough, and safely enough to deploy?

RT-2's 55B parameters run at 1 to 3 Hz on cloud TPUs that cost more than the robot. OpenVLA reports roughly 6 Hz on an RTX 4090, an order of magnitude below the 20 to 50 Hz that dexterous manipulation demands, and that is on a desktop GPU rather than anything that rides onboard. Meanwhile near-term humanoid programs at scale (Tesla Optimus, Agility Digit, Unitree G1) make per-unit inference cost a first-order constraint: when the robot itself costs less than a datacenter GPU, onboard compute is a hard budget rather than a preference.

This workshop treats efficiency as a first-class problem across the whole space of embodied foundation models, vision-language-action policies, world-action models, and emerging architectures alike, rather than any single family. No system today is simultaneously general, fast, and deployable within a real onboard budget. That trilemma is architecture-independent, and it organizes the program.

C1. Architecture and compression across embodied foundation models

What combination of smaller backbones, action chunking, quantization, pruning, distillation, and adaptive compute enables robot control above 20 Hz? Individual speedups span 1.5× to 120×, but no systematic study confirms they compose, or that they transfer between policies and world models.

C2. Edge-cloud co-design across heterogeneous hardware

At ten thousand robots, can you afford a GPU on each one? The accelerator landscape is now heterogeneous: NVIDIA GPUs, Google TPUs, AWS Trainium and Inferentia, mobile NPUs, each with different cost, latency, and energy trade-offs. No published analysis compares total cost of ownership across edge-only, cloud-only, and hybrid architectures at fleet scale.

C3. Safety under latency constraints

Action chunking commits a robot to open-loop execution with no principled interruption mechanism. While the model is still computing, should it freeze, revert to a safe policy, or extrapolate? Certifying systems with stochastic response time remains unsolved.

Call for Papers

We solicit four-page extended abstracts, plus unlimited pages for references, on efficiency across the whole space of embodied foundation models: VLA policies, world-action models, and emerging architectures alike.

Topics

  • Efficient architectures for VLA policies, world-action models, and emerging designs: action chunking, mixture-of-experts, early exit, diffusion and flow step-reduction
  • Compression for robot foundation models: quantization, pruning, distillation
  • Memory-efficient adaptation and fine-tuning
  • Benchmarking methodology, including whether efficiency techniques compose
  • Hardware-aware design across accelerators: GPU, TPU, AWS Trainium and Inferentia, mobile NPUs
  • Edge-cloud hybrid architectures for robot fleets
  • Safety and latency trade-offs, and fallback policies under stochastic response time
  • Efficient world models for online planning and real-time safety monitoring

Submission

Four pages plus unlimited references, hosted on OpenReview, reviewed double-blind. Submissions must be anonymized. New work and previously published work that fits the workshop scope are both eligible, tracked and reviewed separately.

Review and presentation

Each submission receives three reviews from the organizing committee and program committee. All accepted papers are presented as posters, and roughly five are additionally selected for a five-minute lightning talk.

Submission portal and full dates appear under Important Dates.

Embodied Efficiency Challenge

A benchmarking competition producing a concrete, citable artifact. The task is to compress a reference policy until it fits a real onboard budget without losing the capability that made it worth deploying.

Reference model
OpenVLA-7B with a frozen evaluation harness. About 14 GB in bf16, which does not fit a 16 GB Jetson, so quantization, sharding, or offload is required.
Target budget
Jetson Orin NX (16 GB) class hardware. Teams run on their own device.
How it works
We release a reference Docker container and evaluation script. You run it on your hardware and submit code, reported metrics, and a hardware attestation. Organizers re-run the top entries to verify before results are announced, so the leaderboard is reproducibility-checked rather than centrally hosted.
Tasks
LIBERO Spatial, Object, and Goal suites.
Metrics
Success rate first, within 15 points of the published reference. Then median and p99 latency per action. Then parameters and peak memory.
Open to all
The compress-to-a-budget methodology is architecture-agnostic. World-model and emerging-architecture entries are explicitly welcome.
Artifact
All submissions, baselines, and the harness become embodied-efficiency-bench, a permanent open-source resource.
Recognition
Best Efficiency Award and a presentation slot. Travel grant subject to sponsorship.
