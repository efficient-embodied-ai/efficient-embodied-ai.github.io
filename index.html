Efficient Foundation Models for Real-Time Embodied AI

If robot foundation models cannot run fast and cheap, deployment stalls. Efficiency is the gating factor between laboratory demonstration and real-world deployment.

November 12, 2026 Austin, TX Half-Day Workshop
About

The Efficiency Crisis

In 2024, the field asked: can we build robot foundation models? RT-2, Octo, and Open X-Embodiment proved the concept. In 2025, the question shifted to real-world viability — pi0 folded laundry, GR00T N1 controlled humanoids, and OpenVLA made a 7B generalist policy available to any lab. Now a harder question emerges: can they work fast enough, cheaply enough, and safely enough to actually deploy?

RT-2's 55B parameters run at 1–3 Hz on cloud TPUs that cost more than the robot. OpenVLA reports roughly 6 Hz on an RTX 4090, still an order of magnitude below the 20–50 Hz that dexterous manipulation demands, and that is on a desktop GPU rather than anything that rides onboard. Meanwhile near-term humanoid programs at scale (Tesla Optimus, Agility Digit, Unitree G1) make per-unit inference cost a first-order constraint: when the robot itself costs less than a datacenter GPU, onboard compute is a hard budget, not a preference. Every one of these systems needs a foundation model that runs in real time, on affordable hardware, with latency guarantees that satisfy safety requirements.

Research Question 1

Architecture & Compression Across Embodied Foundation Models

What combination of smaller backbones, action chunking, quantization, pruning, distillation, and adaptive compute enables robot control at 20+ Hz? No systematic comparison exists.

Research Question 2

Edge-Cloud Co-Design Across Heterogeneous Hardware

At 10,000 robots, can you afford a GPU on each one? The accelerator landscape is now heterogeneous — NVIDIA GPUs, Google TPUs, AWS Trainium/Inferentia, mobile NPUs — each with different cost, latency, and energy trade-offs. No published analysis compares total cost of ownership across edge-only, cloud-only, and hybrid architectures, or across these accelerators.

Research Question 3

Safety Under Latency Constraints

When a robot's foundation model is still computing, what should it do — freeze, revert to a safe policy, or extrapolate? No principled framework exists for certifying stochastic-latency systems.

Submissions

Call for Papers

We solicit 4-page extended abstracts (plus unlimited pages for references) on efficiency across the whole space of embodied foundation models: VLA policies, world-action models, and emerging architectures alike.

Topics of Interest

  • Efficient architectures for VLA policies, world-action models, and emerging designs (action chunking, MoE, early exit, diffusion/flow step-reduction)
  • Compression for robot foundation models (quantization, pruning, distillation)
  • Memory-efficient adaptation and fine-tuning
  • Benchmarking methodology, including whether efficiency techniques compose
  • Hardware-aware design across accelerators (GPU, TPU, AWS Trainium/Inferentia, mobile NPUs)
  • Edge-cloud hybrid architectures for robot fleets
  • Safety-latency trade-offs and fallback policies under stochastic response time
  • Efficient world models for online planning and real-time safety monitoring

Competition

VLA Efficiency Challenge

A benchmarking competition producing a concrete, citable artifact for the community.

Compress OpenVLA. Beat the Clock.

Reference model: OpenVLA-7B with a frozen evaluation harness (~14GB in bf16, which does not fit a 16GB Jetson, so quantization, sharding, or offload is required).

Target budget: Jetson Orin NX (16GB) class hardware. Teams run on their own device.

How it works: We release a reference Docker container and evaluation script. You run it on your hardware and submit code, reported metrics, and a hardware attestation. Organizers re-run the top entries to verify before results are announced, so the leaderboard is reproducibility-checked rather than centrally hosted.

Tasks: LIBERO Spatial, Object, and Goal suites.

Open to all architectures: the compress-to-a-budget methodology is architecture-agnostic. World-model and emerging-architecture entries are explicitly welcome.

Artifact: All submissions, baselines, and the harness become embodied-efficiency-bench, a permanent open-source community resource.

Recognition: Best Efficiency Award and a presentation slot. Travel grant subject to sponsorship. Top entries present at the workshop.
